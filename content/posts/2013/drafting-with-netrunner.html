This draft has incredibly fun, and I’d recommend netrunner cube to anyone who anyone who likes to draft. It was probably the most fun I’ve ever had playing netrunner. The power level is moderate and the decisions are interesting. I really like mid power level netrunner, since it all feels very balanced, and it can be very viable to stick an agenda in play and advance it and say go. There is no dominance of fast advance strategies.

You can play tricks in your deck and your opponent will have no idea what they are, since there are no influence restrictions. You just have to value them high enough to draft them.

You have to decide whats better: The 3/2 agenda? The Mining Corp? The Tollbooth? This really tests your card evaluation skills!

I hope this catches on, and people start playing netrunner cube draft. Its amazing.

For 2 people drafting, in Netrunner, I think you shouldn’t do any form of ‘face up’ drafting.

If you can see the snares and the junebugs go into their deck, thats going to take away part of the surprise.

Part of whats cool about the netrunner cube draft is that their deck could have anything in it, but only things they managed to draft. If their goal was to hit you with traps and they chose them highly to allow that, you shouldnt know about it, unless you managed to deduce it from the cards you saw.

After some research, I think the following “Pancake draft” method would probably be best for 2 players:

http://blog.garion.org/2013/03/21/pancake-draft-cube-draft-for-two-players/

Basically, in each selection, you are picking a couple cards, and throwing away a couple cards face down. The opponent then gets the remainder and isnt sure which you took and which you discarded.

Therefore, you could draft the snares and throw away something else good. Or you could draft the other cards and throw away the traps. Now your opponent doesnt know which it is.

The only issue with this is that since youre throwing half the cards away, that means you need to draft the whole (4 player) cube for 2. Which means that all of the 3/2 agendas will be in there, and if you are both picking those and not throwing them away, you might both have these super powered agenda mixes of ten 3/2 cards. So some tweaking should be done to fix this.

Hey Thirteen, I will sure try.

““Sit all four players around a table and give them each 3 randomized corp “packs” of 20 cards from your corporate stack. ”

A pack is just a random group of 20 cards from the cube. So basically you combine all of the corp cards together, shuffle them a bunch, and then deal out a “pack” of 20 random cards facedown to form the pack.

You give all four players 3 of these packs, and sit them around a table.

Once that is done you can begin to draft.

“Players will then draft their packs going left-right-left until all three packs have been drafted.”

To draft a pack what you do is simultaneously with all of the other players grab your first pack and look at all of the cards in it. You then choose one of those cards that you want to play with and “draft” it , which means you set it aside as part of your pool of cards that you can deckbuild with later.

You then pass the remaining undrafted cards in the pack to the player on your left. Because the person on your right did the same, you will have a new pack of 19 cards to draft from. You continue this process until you receive just a single passed card as your pack and you draft that.

Then everyone picks up their next pack of cards and does this process again, this time passing to the player on your right instead of the player on your left.

Once the second pack has been completed in the same way as the first pack, players pick up the final third pack, and again draft to the left.

Once all of the corp cards have been drafted you essentially do all of this again except for the Runner cards this time, reversing the order of passing from left-right-left to right-left-right.

Android: Netrunner is an amazing game, but one currently lacking any kind of limited formats similar to drafting or sealed deck in Magic: the Gathering. Anthony and I spent some time thinking about how a drafting format would function with a Living Card Game like Netrunner and eventually found a solution that so far has been incredibly fun.

The format we settled on was a cube-esque draft that ignores influence, allowing you to play with any drafted cards. Currently, with one core set and one of each data pack you can create your own Netrunner Cube that allows drafting with four people.

Imagine looking at a draft pack like this:

 What card would you take for your deck?

 

How does it work?

To create the cube take one core set and combine it with one copy of each data pack currently available. After removing all identities, separate the corporation and runner cards into two separate randomized stacks.

Sit all four players around a table and give them each 3 randomized corp “packs” of 20 cards from your corporate stack. Players will then draft their packs going left-right-left until all three packs have been drafted. Players then repeat this process with 3 randomized runner “packs” of 20 cards , this time alternative right-left-right.

After all packs have been drafted, each player will make a runner and corp deck with the cards that they drafted. Influence and factions are ignored, any card you drafted is legal to play in the appropriate deck (however still no putting runner cards in corp decks or vice versa).

Each player is assumed to have a generic neutral identity that has a 40 card minimum deck size. Alternatively you can put one of each identity into the draft for people to pick as well, or do a separate identity draft of only identities before drafting other cards, but we do not advise this for your first drafts.

While we found it unnecessary, we created an optional rule that allowed anyone without enough agenda points to fill the remaining needed agenda points with proxy agendas that count as 2 points 4 cost with no abilities.

Once everyone’s deck is constructed, sit down and play some of the strangest yet most exciting games you will ever play in Netrunner. Drafting Netrunner is both incredibly difficult and highly entertaining. Thinking about the choice of AstroScript Pilot Program or a Project Atlas or even one of the three Hedge Funds can be incredibly difficult. It is very challenging to try and figure out when you need agendas and how highly you should take them in comparison to both economy cards and ice. Add in the limitations of a core set, do I draft the 1-of SanSan City Grid, or take a more consistently good but more available card like Ice wall or Ichi? Do you take one of the two Astroscript Pilot Programs to give your deck the best agendas, or do you take one of the few Melange Mining Corps to really fuel your economy? All of the choices are incredibly difficult and reward player experience significantly. Here are deck lists and commentary from our most recent draft for your amusement.

Asroybal Overall Comments:
So, what are the keys to drafting good decks? For runner I would say supporting solid aggression is quite strong but you also want to pick up a lot of economy cards in any deck because they allow you to actually play your breakers reasonably fast. For corporation I would say pick up strong ice and economy in general. You can score bad agendas if your ice is good enough and you are not poor.
Moving forward, I would suggest removing identities from the draft pool and drafting them separately before the rest of your draft. Other than that I found the format to be very entertaining and suggest it as an alternative way to play Netrunner for those who enjoy it as whole-heartedly as we do.
